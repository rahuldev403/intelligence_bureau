OPERATION PAPERCLIP — DECLASSIFIED CASE FILE
Bureau Reference: IB-1945-OP7

SUMMARY
Operation Paperclip was a secret United States intelligence program in which more than 1,600
German scientists, engineers, and technicians were recruited from Nazi Germany and brought to
the United States after World War II ended in 1945. The primary purpose was to exploit their
scientific expertise and prevent it from falling into the hands of the Soviet Union, the United
Kingdom, or a revived Germany.

KEY PERSONNEL
- Wernher von Braun: Lead rocket engineer who developed the V-2 ballistic missile for the Third
  Reich. After recruitment, he became the chief architect of NASA's Saturn V rocket, which carried
  Apollo 11 astronauts to the Moon in July 1969.
- Hubertus Strughold: Recruited as a specialist in aviation medicine. Later named "Father of Space
  Medicine" by the USAF. His wartime experiments on concentration camp prisoners were suppressed
  by US authorities during his vetting.
- Walter Dornberger: V-2 program military commander. Recruited by the US Army, later worked for
  Bell Aircraft Corporation.

TIMELINE
1945-MAY    Germany surrenders. Allied intelligence teams race to secure scientists before
            Soviet forces arrive.
1945-JUL    Operation OVERCAST (predecessor to Paperclip) authorised by Joint Chiefs of Staff.
1945-SEP    First group of 88 German specialists arrives in the United States.
1946-MAR    Program renamed Operation Paperclip — named after the paperclip agents attached to
            the personnel files of approved candidates to bypass State Department denials.
1947        President Truman officially sanctions the program despite earlier opposition.
1955        Over 760 German scientists have received US citizenship under the program.
1990        Program details fully declassified and released to the public.

LOCATIONS
- Fort Bliss, Texas: Primary base for von Braun and his rocket team (1945–1950).
- Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio: Aviation medicine and aeronautics recruits.
- Redstone Arsenal, Alabama: Transferred 1950; became the centre of US ballistic missile development.
- White Sands Proving Ground, New Mexico: V-2 launch tests conducted 1946–1951.

OUTCOME
The recruited scientists contributed directly to:
  1. The US ICBM program (Redstone, Jupiter, Pershing missiles).
  2. NASA's space program — Saturn V remains the most powerful rocket ever flown.
  3. Early US research in aviation medicine, radar, and chemical weapons defence.

CONTROVERSY
The State Department and the FBI repeatedly flagged recruits for suspected war crimes and
Nazi Party membership. Joint Intelligence Objectives Agency (JIOA) staff systematically
altered and cleansed dossiers to remove incriminating evidence, allowing candidates to pass
immigration and security reviews. The decision was driven by Cold War strategic priorities
over moral considerations.

RELATED FILES
- IB-1946-SOVIET: Soviet counterpart — Operation OSOAVIAKHIM, which forcibly relocated 2,200
  German scientists to the USSR in a single night on 22 October 1946.
- IB-1969-APOLLO: Moon landing mission files referencing Paperclip recruits at NASA.
